The Hutson (2021) article explores concerning problems with LLM models and Artificial Intelligence (AI) writers, like bias, harmful and even extremist content. The AI-generated content is extremely dangerous; those problems are clear and can be addressed with technology itself, foe example; Balestri (2025) study reveals that newer models could reduce their bias. On the other hand, there is another challenging issue with the use of AI. Are we becoming less smart by letting AI think for us? According to Daniel (2025), yes. Depending too much on generative AI could lead to cognitive offloading and affect our long-term memory. This supports a growing concern: while AI helps us work faster, it might also be making us mentally lazy.

Bishop and Bishop (2024) explains how AI technology works and it states, the risks stem not from the technology itself but from how it is used. Large language models, based on transformer architectures and self-supervised learning, have legitimate potential to augment rather than replace human intelligence. The challenge lies in ensuring that users remain actively engaged in the learning process. In that scenario, I think the bigger risk is our complacency about AI tools.
